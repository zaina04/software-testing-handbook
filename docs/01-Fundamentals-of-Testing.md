# Chapter 1: Fundamentals of Testing

## What is Software Testing?

Software testing is the process of evaluating software to verify that it meets requirements and behaves as expected. The goal is to identify defects before the software reaches users. Testing improves quality, reduces risk, and increases confidence in the product, but it cannot prove that software is completely free of defects.

---

## Why is Testing Important?

Testing helps to:

- Detect defects early
- Reduce development costs
- Improve software quality
- Verify requirements are met
- Reduce business risk
- Increase customer confidence

Example:
A banking app incorrectly transfers money due to a calculation bug. Catching this during testing is far less costly than fixing it after release.

---

## Testing Objectives

The objectives of testing include:

- Finding defects
- Verifying requirements
- Validating user needs
- Preventing defects
- Building confidence in the software
- Supporting decision-making about release readiness

---

## Testing vs Debugging

| Testing | Debugging |
|---------|-----------|
| Finds defects | Finds and fixes the cause of defects |
| Usually performed by testers | Usually performed by developers |
| Focuses on identifying failures | Focuses on correcting defects |

...

## Chapter Summary

- Testing checks whether software behaves as expected.
- Testing reduces risk but cannot prove software is defect-free.
- Testing and debugging are different activities.
- QA focuses on preventing defects, while QC focuses on finding them.
- Understanding the seven testing principles helps create effective test strategies.
